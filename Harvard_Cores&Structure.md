### **The COREs of a Successful Harvard CSE ME SOP**

To stand out, your SOP must adopt these three identities, driven by the specific requirements of the ME degree.

1. **Identity: The "Mini-Thesis" Proposal**
    - **What:** Frame your SOP as a specific research proposal, not just a personal history.
    - **Why:** Unlike the SM degree, the ME requires a thesis defense and 2 years of study1. You must convince the committee you have a viable research plan that necessitates this 2-year timeline, rather than the standard 1-year coursework track.
2. **Identity: The "Computational" Scientist**
    - **What:** Emphasize the *mathematical* and *computational* methods (numerical analysis, simulation, optimization) behind your robotics work, rather than just software engineering.
    - **Why:** The program requires core courses like **AM 205 (Numerical Methods)** and **AM 207 (Stochastic Methods)**22. You must demonstrate that you are seeking "Computational Science" rigor, which distinguishes this program from a general CS degree.
3. **Identity: The "De-Risked" Candidate**
    - **What:** Explicitly highlight your ability to handle math-intensive coursework (Fluid Mechanics, Control Theory) to offset the lack of a GRE score.
    - **Why:** SEAS does not accept GRE scores 3, so your SOP and transcript are the only proof of your quantitative ability to survive the rigorous Applied Math core curriculum4.

### **Task 2: Optimized SOP Structure (The "Weaved" Strategy)**

This structure integrates your **CoRL** (Algorithms) and **OMRON** (Systems) experience to target **Prof. Du** and **Prof. Reddi**.

### **1. Introduction (15%): The Computational Gap**

- **How:** Start with the specific problem from your CoRL research (e.g., geometric policies fail in dynamic environments). State your thesis: You need to fuse **Generative AI** with **Edge Systems** to solve this.
- **Q1 Answer:** **YES, mention professors briefly here.**
    - *Why:* It signals immediately that you are an ME (research) applicant with a clear direction, separating you from the general coursework pool.
    - *Draft:* "...I aim to bridge this gap by integrating **Prof. Yilun Du's** generative frameworks with **Prof. Vijay Janapa Reddi's** edge optimization techniques."

### **2. The "Brain": Algorithmic Research (30%)**

- **How:** Deep dive into your **CoRL 2025** paper. Focus on the *mathematics* of your geometric constraints.
- **Why:** This proves you have the "Research Maturity" required for the ME thesis 5 and aligns with the computational nature of the program. Ends with the limitation: "Geometric rules are insufficient; I need Generative Models."

### **3. The "Body": Systems & Engineering (25%)**

- **How:** Detail your **OMRON** (Distillation) and **Wolley** (Profiling) work. Focus on hardware constraints.
- **Why:** This proves you have the "Engineering" skills to implement physical AI. It sets up the need for System/Hardware research.

### **4. The Harvard Solution: Labs & Courses (20%)**

- **How:** Weave the two threads together.
    - **Lab 1 (Du):** To solve the algorithmic limit (from Sec 2).
    - **Lab 2 (Reddi):** To solve the hardware limit (from Sec 3).
- **Q1 Answer:** **YES, mention specific courses.**
    - *Why:* Citing **AM 207 (Stochastic Methods)** proves you understand the math needed for generative AI, and **CS 2050 (High Performance Computing)** proves you understand the systems needed for edge deployment6. It shows you fit the specific "Computational" curriculum.

### **5. Conclusion (10%): Career Vision**

- **How:** Reiterate your long-term goal (PhD/R&D) and how the ME thesis is the necessary bridge.
- **Why:** Shows you understand the career outcomes of the program (51% Tech, 13% PhD).
