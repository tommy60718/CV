% Body Paragraphs

In my research accepted to \textbf{CoRL 2025}, \emph{``See, Point, Fly,''} I identified a fundamental trade-off in robotic autonomy: geometric rules provide reliability but become brittle in dynamic environments, while end-to-end VLM policies offer adaptability but suffer from hallucinations and high edge latency. My objective is to resolve this tension by architecting \textbf{Physics-Aware, Edge-Native Autonomy}---systems that constrain the creativity of VLMs with the rigor of physics and the efficiency of silicon. I specifically seek the \textbf{Cyber-Physical Systems} track at UCLA to achieve this, as it uniquely offers the convergence of \textbf{Prof. Achuta Kadambi’s} computational imaging priors and \textbf{Prof. Ankur Mehta’s} embedded optimization techniques.

My preparation is defined by the convergence of \textbf{Mechanical Engineering} first principles and \textbf{Computer Science} algorithmic reasoning. Unlike black-box approaches, my CoRL work formulated a \textbf{learning-free} control framework. I utilized \textbf{Projective Geometry} to derive non-linear unprojection laws, mapping 2D pixel space directly to \textbf{SE(3) velocity primitives}. This formulation solved depth ambiguity without external sensors, optimizing visual tracking complexity from $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N)$. Beyond the technical derivation, I \textbf{led the manuscript composition} and delivered a \textbf{spotlight presentation} at CoRL 2025, honing my ability to distill complex theoretical concepts for a global audience.

However, I observed that static pin-hole assumptions fail in unstructured environments with complex light transport. The system lacks \textbf{differentiable optical priors}---a ``Physics Gap'' I aim to resolve at UCLA.

Simultaneously, my research philosophy is defined by the constraints of the physical layer. While many focus solely on algorithms, my internship at \textbf{Wolley Inc.} grounded me in hardware architecture. I engineered C-based diagnostic tools to profile \textbf{CXL Type-3 memory} bandwidth and host pressure, gaining the low-level intuition needed to optimize I/O efficiency for physical AI. I plan to extend this hardware-software co-design mindset during my \textbf{upcoming internship at OMRON SINIC X}, where I will distill Robotics Foundation Models for edge deployment. I possess a full-stack perspective: optimizing both the high-level policy and the low-level memory architecture to bridge the ``Resource Gap.''

UCLA is the ideal environment to synthesize these tracks. To solve ``Physical Blindness,'' I aim to join the \textbf{Visual Machines Group}. I propose replacing static heuristics with \textbf{learnable optical priors} embedded in VLM attention layers, aligning with the ``VLM4D'' vision. To address the ``Computational Deadlock,'' I seek \textbf{Prof. Ankur Mehta’s LEMUR Lab} to co-design hardware-aware compression techniques, enabling heavy foundation models to run on ubiquitous robots.

To support this research, I require a rigorous theoretical upgrade. My background prepares me for \textbf{ECE 209AS (Computational Imaging)} to formalize my optical intuition, and \textbf{ECE 236B (Convex Optimization)} to transform my heuristic safety checks into provably stable guarantees. My record of zero-shot SOTA results demonstrates methodological velocity; I seek the rigor of the UCLA ecosystem to refine my theoretical depth and define the safety guarantees required for the next generation of Trustworthy Embodied Systems.
